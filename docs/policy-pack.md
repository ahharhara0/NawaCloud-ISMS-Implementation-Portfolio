# Policy and Procedure Pack (Draft)

These original drafts belong to a fictional scenario. They are not approved policies for a real organization. A real organization must assign owners, obtain management approval, and define scope, retention, and review before use.

## 1. Information Security Policy
NawaCloud commits in this simulation to protect customer information and service availability, assess and treat risks, assign responsibilities and resources, and review objectives, incidents, audits, and improvement actions. The policy applies to personnel, contractors, and suppliers within their responsibilities. Access and disclosure require an approved business need. Personnel promptly report suspected security events through the incident channel. Management reviews the policy annually and after material change.

## 2. Identity and Access Procedure
Managers request access through a recorded ticket that states role, duration, and approval. Each person uses a unique account; privileged accounts use multifactor authentication. Shared accounts are prohibited. Access is reviewed quarterly and on role change, and removed on the departure date. Exceptions are temporary, justified, approved by the system owner, and have an expiry date.

## 3. Secure Development and Change Procedure
Changes are recorded before implementation with security impact, tests, and rollback steps. A second person reviews high-impact changes. Development, test, and production environments and deployment permissions are separated where practical. Secrets are prohibited in source code; dependencies and vulnerabilities are checked according to risk. Test data is synthetic or sanitized. Release source, approval, and test results are recorded.

## 4. Incident Response
Prepare roles, internal contact channels, dependencies, and suppliers. Record discovery time and affected systems without placing sensitive data in public channels. The incident lead classifies confidentiality, integrity, availability, and obligation impacts. Contain affected accounts or services and preserve logs where practical. Remove the cause, rotate credentials when needed, verify service integrity, and restore. Authorized personnel decide on notifications after reviewing applicable duties. Record root cause and assigned actions. This is a training sequence; no real incident occurred.

## 5. Backup and Continuity
Backups are encrypted under a retention policy approved by the data owner. Permissions are restricted and backup copies are separated from routine operational access. Illustrative targets are RPO 4 hours and RTO 8 hours. Restoration is tested quarterly in an isolated test environment, with results and gaps recorded. Recovery order and providers are reviewed annually.

## 6. Supplier Management
Before contracting, classify suppliers by data, service, access, and operational dependency. Agreements should define security, confidentiality, incident notification, subcontractors, data return/deletion, and assurance information where applicable. Review assurance annually or after material change. On termination, remove accounts, return or delete data as agreed, and document access closure.

## 7. Awareness
New personnel receive awareness before production data access. Topics include phishing, authentication, data classification, file sharing, incident reporting, and remote work. Refresh annually and after material change or incident. Measure completion and understanding with non-punitive exercises and use results to improve content. Collect only necessary personal information.
